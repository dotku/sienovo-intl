#### ▎Introduction

The RK3568 and the Raspberry Pi 4 are two of the most widely used ARM platforms in embedded computing. This article compares them on processor performance, expandability, power consumption and software support, including CODESYS PLC runtimes, to show where each one fits.

#### ▎RK3568

Rockchip announced the RK3568 at its 2020 developer conference. It is built on a 22nm process and has a quad-core Cortex-A55 CPU (up to 2.0GHz), a Mali-G52 2EE GPU (800MHz), a built-in NPU rated at 1 TOPS, and a wide range of high-speed interfaces. It runs Linux and Android and targets industrial markets such as IoT gateways, industrial HMI panels and in-vehicle systems.

#### ▎Raspberry Pi 4

Released in 2019, the Raspberry Pi 4 uses the Broadcom BCM2711: a 28nm quad-core Cortex-A72 CPU (1.5GHz, 1.8GHz on later board revisions) with a VideoCore VI GPU (500MHz). It runs Raspberry Pi OS, Ubuntu and other distributions, and is widely used for media centers, IoT prototypes, robotics and education.

#### ▎Processor Performance

The Cortex-A72 in the Raspberry Pi 4 is a high-performance out-of-order core, while the Cortex-A55 in the RK3568 is an efficiency-focused in-order core. Clock for clock the A72 is considerably faster, so the Raspberry Pi 4 is ahead in both single-thread and multi-thread CPU benchmarks despite the RK3568's higher clock speed.

The RK3568 makes up for this in other areas. Its 1 TOPS NPU runs quantized vision models (object detection, classification) without loading the CPU, which the Raspberry Pi 4 cannot do without an external accelerator. Its Mali-G52 GPU is also stronger than the VideoCore VI for graphics workloads.

In practice: for CPU-heavy general computing the Raspberry Pi 4 is faster; for edge AI inference and industrial I/O, the RK3568 is the better fit.

#### ▎Expandability

The RK3568 offers PCIe 3.0, SATA, dual Gigabit Ethernet, USB 3.0, MIPI DSI/CSI, LVDS, eDP and HDMI. PCIe allows NVMe SSDs, and core boards usually ship with onboard eMMC, which is more reliable than an SD card in industrial environments.

The Raspberry Pi 4 provides USB 3.0, GPIO, dual micro-HDMI, CSI and a single Gigabit Ethernet port. It boots from microSD or USB storage, but the standard board has no eMMC and no exposed PCIe slot (the Compute Module 4 variant adds both).

#### ▎Power Consumption

The Cortex-A55 cores and 22nm process give the RK3568 good efficiency, with low idle power and modest heat at moderate loads, which suits fanless enclosures. The Raspberry Pi 4's A72 cores draw more power and run hotter under sustained load, and usually need a heatsink or fan in enclosed cabinets.

#### ▎Software Support and CODESYS

The Raspberry Pi 4 has the largest community and software ecosystem of any ARM board, which makes it easy for beginners and fast for prototyping. CODESYS offers an official **CODESYS Control for Raspberry Pi** runtime, so it is a popular low-cost way to learn IEC 61131-3 PLC programming.

The RK3568 runs Linux (Debian, Ubuntu, Buildroot) and Android, with vendor BSPs aimed at commercial products. It can run the **CODESYS Control for Linux ARM64** runtime, which combined with its dual Ethernet, wide-temperature core board options and onboard eMMC makes it better suited to production industrial controllers, for example a soft PLC that also handles HMI display and edge AI on one device.

#### ▎Conclusion

Both platforms have clear strengths. The Raspberry Pi 4 has the faster CPU cores and an unmatched community, which makes it ideal for education, prototyping and hobby projects, including learning CODESYS on a low budget.

The RK3568 trades some raw CPU speed for a built-in NPU, richer industrial I/O (dual Ethernet, PCIe, SATA, multiple display interfaces), onboard eMMC and lower power. For production edge devices, such as soft PLCs, industrial gateways, HMIs and AI vision boxes, it is usually the more practical choice.
